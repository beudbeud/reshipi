My Kodak Gold 200 recipe for Fuji X and GFX cameras

Mar 27
Written By Scott Tucker

Check out all of my film sims here

Unsatisfied with a Fuji Gold 200 film sim from others, I decided to make my own. This was developed on an X100V and needs a few settings from that generation of cameras to get the most out of it. It's best shot in the morning and evening when light is just starting to turn gold, golden hour is almost too much but you can tone down the WB value to 4800 or so if needed for golden hour shooting.

I find it's better to overexpose this one by 1/3 to 2/3 of a stop to get the gold tones more prominent. Dynamic range between light and shadow is prioritized for a more filmic look.

I also developed a Lightroom Classic preset to be applied over the JPEG output to make the colors pop a bit more, linked below. As always, my presets are completely free. No one should be charging you money to move a few sliders. That said, I will gladly accept donations (link below) if you feel this preset is of value to you after you try it out.

Scott - Over Gold 200 daytime.xmp

I have also developed a set of 3 Lightroom presets to be used on Fuji GFX RAW files to get you extremely close to the out-of-camera look of the recipe, but with the added latitude of shooting RAW. You lose the benefits of shooting DR400 in JPEG, but these will get you very close. I have not tested on Fuji X RAW files.

Gold 200 is the base preset
Gold 200 Hazy adds a softness that is similar to the clarity setting in Fuji cameras
Gold 200 Outdoor Portrait is a bit more forgiving on skin

I tend to keep the WB value somewhere about halfway between cloudy and daylight depending on the light and time of day. You will likely need to fiddle with WB to your preference when editing. The only other adjustment you need to make is Exposure, but all of the colors should be very close to the in-cam recipe.

Scott GFX Gold 200 LR Presets.zip

The in-camera receipe:


Film Sim: Classic Neg (if you don't have Classic Neg, I'd start at Pro Neg Hi and work your way from there)
Grain Effect: Strong Small
Color Chrome Effect: Strong
Color Chrome FX Blue: Strong
White Balance: 5100K, R:2, B:-2
DR: DR400
D Range: off
Tone Curve: H:-2, S:-2
Color: +4
Sharpness: 0
Noise Reduction: -2
Clarity: 0

Edit: it has been brought to my attention how similar this recipe is to Classic Cuban Neg, which came out about a year after this one was first published on the internet. The main differences is I retain bright areas as golden where Cuban turns more brown, and my shadow areas are significantly brighter. I suggest trying both, as the higher contrast Cuban can work better in certain situations. Overall, I think mine is the more balanced of the two for more shooting situations, but I’m biased =)
